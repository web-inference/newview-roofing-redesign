Recognizing a poorly installed roof is crucial in preventing further damage to your home. Some clear signs can alert you to potential issues. For instance, you might see shingles that are not aligned properly or seem to be lifting at the edges. This can lead to leaks. Gaps in the flashing around chimneys or vent pipes are another common sign. These gaps can allow water to seep into your home, causing damage over time. Pay special attention after the first few storms with your new roof. Improper installation is especially noticeable during or after rain.

If you suspect your roof has been improperly installed, the first step is to contact the original roofing company. It’s important to approach this conversation with a clear and calm demeanor. Provide them with specific details about the issues you’ve observed. If possible, take clear photos of the problem areas to support your claims. A responsible roofing company will take your concerns seriously and should be willing to inspect the roof and address the problems. Keep a record of all communications with the company, as this can be important if the issue escalates and you need to seek further assistance or legal advice.

Insurance & Cost

Generally, roof repairs or replacement can fall under your policy’s dwelling coverage, covering the physical structure of your home. It’s a component of your overall homeowner’s insurance and is often contingent on the age and condition of the roof at the time the policy was purchased or subsequently renewed.

Some insurance carriers may consider the age of the roof when determining available coverage, subject to policy terms and underwriting guidelines. This means that if your roof sustains damage after the benchmark, the insurance provider may only offer a depreciated cost, often referred to as ‘actual cash value’ (ACV).

Materials & Systems

Understanding the anatomy of a complete roofing system is crucial for any homeowner, especially when considering repairs or replacements. At New View Roofing, we believe in educating our clients about the various components that make up a roofing system. This knowledge not only helps in making informed decisions but also in maintaining the roof’s integrity over time. In this guide, we’ll break down the different elements of a roofing system and explain their functions.

Read the full answer

The roof decking, or sheathing, is the foundation upon which the entire roofing system is built. Typically made from plywood or oriented strand board (OSB), the decking provides a flat, sturdy surface for other roofing materials to be installed on. It also plays a crucial role in supporting the roof’s structure and distributing weight evenly.

The underlayment is a layer of material placed on the roof decking before installing shingles or tiles. It acts as a secondary barrier against water and weather elements, protecting the decking from moisture that can seep through the outer roofing materials. There are various types of underlayment, including felt and synthetic products, each offering different levels of protection and durability.

A roof isn’t just a cover for your home. It provides protection from the elements, impacts your energy efficiency, and contributes to the overall aesthetic of your house. For Dallas homeowners, selecting the appropriate roofing material can mean the difference between costly repairs and long-term peace of mind.

Asphalt shingles are one of the most popular roofing materials in the U.S. They are known for their durability and can withstand the varying temperatures and weather conditions experienced in Dallas.

If you’re like many people, you know that taking care of your roof is important – and the better care you take of it now, the longer it’ll last. In fact, with proper maintenance and care, you may be able to make your asphalt roof last longer and push out a roof replacement for a few (or several) years.

You should have your roof professionally inspected every year, even if you don’t think there are any problems with it. A qualified roofing contractor will know what to look for and can spot potential issues before they become big (and expensive) problems.

TPO (thermoplastic polyolefin) and PVC (polyvinyl chloride) are thermoplastic single-ply roofing membranes that have heat-welded seams. Both are considered ‘cool’ roofing materials and may help improve energy efficiency and reduce cooling demands. They’re also installed using a similar process.

The differences between the TPO and PVC materials lie in their chemical formulas. Both are designed to conform to the movement of the roof; however, PVC and TPO have different performance characteristics. PVC is often selected for applications where chemical resistance is a priority and may offer greater flexibility in certain applications. TPO and PVC have different cost and performance characteristics, and TPO is often selected when project budget is an important consideration, and TPO and PVC have different thermal expansion characteristics.

A common myth is that metal roofs are noisy, especially during rainstorms. In reality, modern metal roofs are installed with solid sheathing and insulation, which dampens sound. When installed correctly, metal roofs are no noisier than other roofing materials. The underlayment and insulation create a sound barrier, making the noise levels similar to those of asphalt shingles.

Another myth is that metal roofs attract lightning. Metal roofs do not increase the likelihood of a lightning strike. In fact, metal is non-combustible and can dissipate electrical charges, making it a safe option during a lightning storm. Even better, a metal roof can actually protect your home by reducing the risk of fire caused by lightning.

Your gutters form part of your roofing system’s first line of defense against the elements. By diverting rainwater away from your roof, the gutter system protects it from moisture damage. Of course, not all kinds of gutters can provide the same level of protection. In general, seamless gutters can offer more protection than conventional gutters because they’re less likely to suffer leaks.

The joints or area where gutter sections meet are a weak point of traditional gutters systems and the part where leaks usually form. Since seamless gutters, as the name suggests, don’t have any gutter joints, they’re less susceptible to leaks. By installing more durable seamless gutters, you can save on gutter and roof repair costs.
